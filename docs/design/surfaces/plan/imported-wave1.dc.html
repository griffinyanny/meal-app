Still open in C

Three things the density test exposes.

1 · Gold budget. One rationale per dinner is five gold marks in a three-meal scroll. Either rationale collapses to tap-to-reveal below dinner, or the week view shows it only on the next two days.

2 · Where tonight sits. Pinned at the top reads well on Wednesday; on Sunday morning it is the first day of the rail anyway. Worth checking what 10am Wednesday looks like, when tonight is not yet urgent.

3 · Rating at volume. Three dinners is a row of thumbs. Eighteen meals is a screen of its own — the “Rate them” door needs a destination.

4 · Tapping a day. At three meals a day the day group is the natural tap target, but we have not drawn what it opens — a day sheet holding all its meals, or the group expanding in place with the other days pushed down. Worth answering before the sheet explorations settle, because a day sheet and a meal sheet would share a shell.

Not in C, on purpose

Food photography. It is still the biggest warmth lever and it changes the day group’s geometry — at three meals a day, thumbnails may be the only way lunch and breakfast stay scannable.

Drag-to-move is drawn as a grip but not designed as an interaction; non-contiguous weeks fit the rail unchanged but are not shown.

Both are cheap to add once the state matrix lands.

ADirection A · the chef’s proposal

A week you accept

The chef’s case is the first object on the screen and it holds the decision. Days are labels on cards; the nights are evidence for the argument above them.

Optimises for

Trust. The judgement is the payload, and it arrives at full weight before a single meal is read.

Costs you

Structure. A day is a label, so an empty night, a moved meal and a four-day week are things the layout tolerates rather than states it expresses.

Commits us to

BDirection B · the week, assembled

Optimises for

Control. Out, empty, moved and “not here Monday” are all things the screen can already say.

Costs you

Voice. The chef’s case is one italic line above a rail; at the moment of confirming, the argument is thinner than the machinery.

Commits us to

A manipulable object. Drag, add-a-night and leftovers that follow their source all have a home. The risk is Plan reading as a schedule.

How it resolved

Structure from B, voice from A — and B is the one that survives four meals a day.

B’s rail can host A’s voice — a summary at feature size is a type change. A’s card list cannot grow a dated spine, leftover dependencies and drag without becoming B. Voice is portable; structure is not.

At breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks, A becomes twenty-eight argued cards a week and the argument breaks; B’s day already groups by the right key, so a day becomes a container and the meals become rows. Built as Direction C above.

If you pick A instead

It is the right call if you believe the first ten seconds of Plan decide whether the product is believed, and that most weeks are accepted rather than rearranged.

Then take two things from B: real dates on the day labels, and first-class Eating out / Nothing planned cards — so the week is still a set of chosen days, not a stack of five recipes.

Deferred either way: drag-to-move, and non-contiguous weeks.
